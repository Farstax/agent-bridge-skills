# Offer Design

## Job

Turn a positioning statement into a concrete offer a customer can say yes or
no to: what they get, what it costs, what removes their risk.

## Trigger

Use once a position exists, before writing landing-page or sales copy.

## Required inputs

- The positioning statement and the specific pain point it addresses.
- What solving the problem is actually worth to the customer (time, money,
  or risk avoided) — ask for this if not already known; don't estimate it
  silently.

## Method

1. State what the customer is buying as an outcome, not a feature list: the
   named result, not "10 modules" or "a dashboard."
2. Set a price and anchor it against a real, verifiable alternative cost
   (an agency rate, a competitor's price, the cost of the status quo) — never
   an invented anchor.
3. Define risk reversal the business can actually honor. Only offer a
   guarantee the operator has confirmed they can fulfil.
4. If bonuses are relevant, tie each one to the next problem the customer
   will hit after buying — not an arbitrary add-on.
5. Do not add scarcity or urgency language unless it's true (a real seat
   limit, a real deadline the operator has confirmed). Do not manufacture
   urgency to increase conversion.

## Evidence requirements

Price anchors, guarantees, and any specific claim ("saves N hours") must be
confirmed by the operator or drawn from real product data — not invented for
persuasive effect.

## Outputs

An offer summary: mechanism/outcome, price, anchor, guarantee, and any
evidence-backed bonuses — ready to hand to `conversion-copy`.

## Failure / insufficient evidence

If the operator can't confirm they can honor a guarantee or bonus, drop it
rather than including an offer term the business can't deliver.

## Dependencies and effects

No external service required. This Skill produces a draft offer only; it does
not publish pricing or bind the business to any term. Confirm the final offer
with the operator before it goes live anywhere.
