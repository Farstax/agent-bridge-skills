# Product Specification

## Job and trigger
Use for product discovery synthesis, feature briefs, user journeys and prioritised PRD/specification drafts. This is product-scoping work, not a substitute for a repository's `requirements-to-acceptance`, `delivery-directives` or implementation-review process.

## Method
1. State user, problem, evidence, goal and non-goals. Keep research-derived observations distinct from opinion and unvalidated assumptions.
2. Describe current and desired journey, key flows, roles/permissions, important empty/error/retry states and constraints. Reuse product vocabulary and established UI patterns where supplied.
3. Record a small number of meaningful acceptance criteria, measurable outcomes and risks that change a product decision. Do not prescribe code or architecture where an existing project convention owns it.
4. Surface unresolved product choices (with up to two grounded options when necessary). Limit a first draft to the information required to make the next decision.
5. Produce a copyable/editable specification or requested `docx`/`pdf` artifact; avoid building a new project-management integration.

## Boundaries
Do not invent customer interviews, analytics, technical guarantees or business approval. No automatic issue creation, deployment or public roadmap publication unless explicitly requested.
