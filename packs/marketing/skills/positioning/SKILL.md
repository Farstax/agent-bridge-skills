# Positioning

## Job

Turn research findings into one clear position: who this is for, what gap it
fills, and why a competitor can't credibly claim the same space.

## Trigger

Use once pain points and competitive gaps exist (from `market-research` or
equivalent existing data), before writing offer, brand, or conversion copy.

## Required inputs

- Named pain points and competitive gaps (from `market-research` or the
  operator's own knowledge).
- Who the audience actually is — not a generic demographic, a specific
  situation ("solo consultants billing under $10k/month," not "small
  businesses").

## Method

1. Check the position against three questions: Is it urgent (why does this
   matter now)? Is it specific to a named audience? Is the mechanism —
   what's actually different about the approach — stated plainly?
2. Map where competitors sit on the two or three axes that matter for this
   market (for example price vs. complexity, or DIY vs. done-for-you). Find
   the gap, not a crowded position dressed up as different.
3. State the position as one sentence a customer could repeat back: "the
   [specific mechanism] for [specific audience] who [specific situation]."
4. Draft two or three candidate angles and note the concrete evidence
   (from research) each one is grounded in.

## Evidence requirements

Every angle must trace back to a research finding or a verifiable product
fact. Do not invent a mechanism, differentiator, or competitor weakness that
research didn't establish.

## Outputs

A one-sentence position statement, the competitive map it's derived from, and
2-3 ranked candidate angles with their supporting evidence.

## Failure / insufficient evidence

If research is thin or absent, ask for it (or run `market-research` first)
rather than guessing at a position from assumption.

## Dependencies and effects

No external service required. Local reasoning over supplied or
previously-gathered research.
