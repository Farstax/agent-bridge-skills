# Email Sequences

## Job

Design a sequence of emails toward one goal (deliver value, build trust, get
a specific action), using the brand voice and grounded in the offer.

## Trigger

Use once brand voice and offer exist, when building a welcome flow, ongoing
nurture, launch sequence, or cart-recovery flow.

## Required inputs

- Brand voice profile, offer, and the sequence's single goal (deliver a lead
  magnet, build trust before a launch, recover an abandoned action).
- Sending platform/schedule constraints from the operator, if any — do not
  assume a fixed day or time; ask, or leave scheduling to the platform.

## Method

1. Pick a structure that matches the goal: a short welcome flow that
   delivers the promised value first and explains the mechanism next; an
   ongoing nurture mix weighted toward value over pitches; a launch sequence
   that builds logic, then relevance, then a real (not fabricated) close;
   a recovery flow that helps before it reminds.
2. Each email needs one job. State it before drafting.
3. Subject lines should earn curiosity honestly — no clickbait that the body
   doesn't deliver on.
4. Do not manufacture urgency (fake countdown framing, invented scarcity).
   If a deadline or limited bonus is real, state it plainly.
5. Avoid spam-trigger patterns (ALL CAPS, excessive "free"/"guarantee"
   repetition) as a deliverability concern, not a persuasion tactic.

## Evidence requirements

Any claim, result, or guarantee mentioned in an email must match the
confirmed offer — don't introduce a new claim that offer-design or the
operator hasn't validated.

## Outputs

A sequence outline (each email's job and send trigger) plus drafted copy per
email, flagged for operator review before scheduling.

## Failure / insufficient evidence

If the sequence's goal or the offer it supports isn't clear, ask before
drafting rather than guessing at a generic flow.

## Dependencies and effects

No specific email platform is required — this Skill produces copy and an
outline for whatever sending tool the operator uses. It does not send email,
create automations, or access subscriber data.
